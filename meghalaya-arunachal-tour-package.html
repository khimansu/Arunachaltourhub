Meghalaya + Arunachal tour package

Nine nights across two very different states — Cherrapunji's root bridges and waterfalls first, then straight up into the Eastern Himalaya.

Meghalaya is wet, green and dramatic at low altitude. Arunachal is dry, high and Buddhist. Putting them in one trip gives you more variety than any other combination in the North East, and both are reached from the same airport.

The cost is road time: Shillong to Dirang is a long day even with an early start. We build a night at Guwahati or Nameri into the middle of the trip rather than pretending it can be driven in one go.

Why combine a Meghalaya Arunachal tour

A Meghalaya Arunachal tour makes sense because both start and end at Guwahati and the gap between them is one drive to Nameri. You get two very different mountain landscapes — the wet green plateau of Meghalaya and the high Himalayan passes of Arunachal — for the price of one flight.

The root bridge trek is real

The double-decker bridge at Nongriat is roughly 3,000 steps down and the same back up, in humid air. It takes most people four to six hours round trip. If anyone in your group has knee trouble or is not comfortable with sustained stairs, we substitute the single-decker bridge at Riwai, which is a ten-minute walk and still worth seeing.

On the pace

Day five is a long transfer. There is no way around it — Shillong and Tawang are 700 km apart with Guwahati in between. If you would rather not spend a day in the car, we can fly you nowhere useful, so the alternative is to drop Cherrapunji and take the Assam and Arunachal trip instead.

Frequently asked questions

Can Meghalaya and Arunachal be done in one trip?

Yes, in ten days or more. Both are reached from Guwahati, but they are in opposite directions, so budget one full transfer day in the middle.

Do I need a permit for Meghalaya?

No. Meghalaya has no Inner Line Permit requirement. You only need the ILP for the Arunachal portion, which we arrange.

When is the best time for this combined trip?

October to April. Meghalaya is spectacular in the monsoon but the Arunachal half becomes risky, so the two halves only overlap comfortably in the dry season.
